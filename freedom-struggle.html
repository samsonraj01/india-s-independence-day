Strategies of Resistance

How They Fought for Freedom

India's liberation movement was not a single monolithic event. It utilized a wide spectrum of methods, from passive resistance to armed organization.

✊ Non-violent & Civil Disobedience

Led primarily by Mahatma Gandhi, this method focused on breaking unjust British laws peacefully (e.g. producing salt, refusing to pay oppressive taxes) and courting arrest to expose the moral bankruptcy of the colonial administration.

🌾 Boycott & Swadeshi

Economic warfare was waged by boycotting British goods, clothes, and institutions, and promoting Indian-made products (Swadeshi), particularly hand-spun Khadi cloth. This hit the economic heart of the British Empire.

⚡ Revolutionary & Armed Resistance

Organized by figures like Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, and Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose. It involved underground networks, armed uprisings, and military campaigns (such as the INA), demonstrating that Indians would fight physically for their rights.

📰 Press & Literature

Nationalist papers (like Bal Gangadhar Tilak's *Kesari*) and patriotic poetry (like Subramania Bharati's verses) bypassed colonial censorship to raise public awareness, exposing famines, tax abuses, and calling for absolute self-rule (*Swaraj*).

🏹 Tribal & Peasant Movements

Ordinary farmers and Adivasi communities rose up against British forest acts and high rent demands. Led by local leaders like Birsa Munda and Alluri Sitarama Raju, they engaged in guerrilla warfare in forest territories.

🛠️ Students, Peasants & Labor Unions

Millions of students walked out of British colleges, and labor unions organized massive railway and factory strikes. This halted colonial infrastructure, proving that the struggle was driven by the working class.

Courage & Leadership

Women in the Freedom Movement

Women were not just passive supporters; they led major demonstrations, managed secret radios, commanded military wings, and faced long prison sentences.

👑
Begum Hazrat Mahal Awadh (1820 – 1879)

Leader of the 1857 Uprising in Lucknow

Role: Queen Regent of Awadh who defied British annexation.

Major Contribution: She took charge of Lucknow during the Great Revolt of 1857, reorganized the rebel army, and successfully coordinated resistance alongside Tatya Tope against British forces.

Legacy: Famously refused British pensions and chose exile in Nepal to remain free. She remains an early symbol of royal female defiance against colonial rule.

👵
Kasturba Gandhi Gujarat (1869 – 1944)

Pioneer of Satyagraha & Women Mobilization

Role: Political activist and educator.

Major Contribution: Led civil rights protests in South Africa and coached women satyagrahis in India. When Mahatma Gandhi was jailed, she repeatedly stepped up to lead mass campaigns, maintaining the movement's momentum.

Legacy: Suffered severe bronchial illnesses under harsh imprisonment, eventually passing away under British detention in the Aga Khan Palace in 1944.

📻
Usha Mehta Gujarat / Maharashtra (1920 – 2000)

Voice of the Underground Resistance

Role: Freedom fighter and broadcaster.

Major Contribution: Established and operated the secret 'Congress Radio' transmitter during the Quit India Movement of 1942, broadcasting speeches and news when the British shut down the national press.

Legacy: Arrested and kept in solitary confinement; refused to betray her co-conspirators. In 1998, the Government of India awarded her the Padma Vibhushan.

🎖️
Captain Lakshmi Sahgal Kerala / Singapore (1914 – 2012)

Commander of the Rani of Jhansi Regiment

Role: Medical doctor and INA military officer.

Major Contribution: Commanded the Rani of Jhansi Regiment, an all-women combat infantry unit raised by Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose in 1943. She led recruitment, medical relief, and active warfare operations in Burma.

Legacy: Captured by British forces in 1945 and brought back to India as a national hero. She continued her public service as a doctor and politician in independent India.
